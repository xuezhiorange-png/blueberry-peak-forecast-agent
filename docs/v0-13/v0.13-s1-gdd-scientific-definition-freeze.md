## Decision and scope

Select **V0_13_GDD_7C_SIMPLE_MEAN_R1**: one predeclared 7.0°C research threshold,
not a universally calibrated blueberry physiological constant. Its role is
`PREDECLARED_HIGHBUSH_SHB_RESEARCH_HEAT_ACCUMULATION_THRESHOLD`.
This decision answers which representation to test; it does not answer whether
that representation improves forecasts. No model scores were consulted.

The daily definition is:

```text
DAILY_MEAN_C = (TMAX_C + TMIN_C) / 2
DAILY_GDD = max(0, DAILY_MEAN_C - 7.0)
```

Use the accepted frozen ERA5 fields `sampled_local_day_tmax_c` and
`sampled_local_day_tmin_c`, in Celsius, for local days in Asia/Shanghai.
Do not substitute the ERA5 hourly-derived daily mean, preclip Tmin, compare
alternative mean formulas, or select an algorithm by validation performance.
This is an explicit Tmax/Tmin convention compatible with the existing daily
fields, not a reconstruction of canopy temperature.

There is no upper cap. This does **not** imply unlimited biological benefit from
high temperatures: the feature is a simple heat-accumulation representation,
not a calibrated nonlinear physiological forcing kernel. Keep U014 unresolved.

## Scientific evidence and reconciliation

The [machine-readable source register](evidence/v0.13-s1-gdd-source-register.json)
records all requested A–E evidence directions, including crop type, cultivar,
stage, base, cap, formula, origin and applicability. Unknown details remain
explicitly unknown. Several publisher open requests returned 403; verified
primary claims were available in publisher-indexed abstract/method/table text.
The 2025 review was accessible through its university repository. We do not
claim unrestricted full-text verification of every paper.

- **A — Kirk & Isaacs (2012):** highbush flower-development thresholds around
  7–8°C support the agronomic neighborhood of 7°C. This is flowering evidence,
  not a universal harvest-response constant.
  [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.21273/HORTSCI.47.9.1291).
- **B — NeSmith (2012):** seven SHB cultivars across Georgia sites used a 7°C
  heat-unit base from 50% flowering to 50% ripening. Timing errors improved for
  most cultivars but cultivar/site variability remained. **Method difference:**
  Tmin below 7°C was raised to 7°C *before* averaging. S1 does not reproduce
  that clipping rule or its bloom anchor; the paper supports threshold precedent,
  not exact equivalence to our simple final-zero-clipped formula.
  [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1080/15538362.2011.619430).
- **C — Carlson & Hancock (1991):** Michigan first-picking work demonstrates
  dependence on cultivar, accumulation start and low/high thresholds. Its
  historical 72-combination optimization is not permission to repeat a sweep
  on our consumed validation data.
  [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.21273/JASHS.116.5.774).
- **D — Li & Bi (2019):** SHB high-tunnel production independently used 7°C.
  Its Table 1 uses monthly mean-temperature heat units, not our daily formula.
  It supports a practical convention, not an exact daily-method replication.
  [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.21273/HORTSCI13639-18).
- **E — Paredes et al. (2025):** Table 12 retains highbush 5.6°C and 7°C
  entries and a separate rabbiteye 7°C entry; blueberry upper thresholds are
  not reported. This synthesis supports contextual interpretation, not averaging
  all thresholds into one universal parameter.
  [University-hosted review](https://ruidera.uclm.es/server/api/core/bitstreams/eadbfcfb-c6ef-4a90-b809-25d22e8dc17b/content).

Together, A/B/D provide sufficient highbush/SHB precedent for the owner-proposed
fixed research threshold. Differences in crop type, stage, production system and
method remain limitations. S1 is not proving other thresholds wrong. Values such
as 4.4°C or 10°C in other contexts are not silent replacement authorities here;
no business botanical-type authority requires them, and no performance-driven
threshold selection is permitted. A list of possible temperatures is not an
experiment configuration.

## Frozen windows, missingness and precision

| Feature | Inclusive source dates |
| --- | --- |
| GDD_W7 | origin − 7 through origin − 1 |
| GDD_W14 | origin − 14 through origin − 1 |
| GDD_W30 | origin − 30 through origin − 1 |

Never include the origin day or future realized temperature. For an origin of
2025-12-10 00:00 Asia/Shanghai, the latest eligible source date is 2025-12-09.
These event-time rules do not establish historical as-issued availability.

Missing any required day makes the row ineligible. Do not interpolate, forward
fill, monthly-average fill or zero fill. Missing/nonfinite temperatures,
duplicate/wrong local days or Tmax < Tmin also fail closed; do not silently
accept Kelvin. S2 must audit coverage before any scientific scoring.

Use Decimal arithmetic with context precision at least 50 for the intermediate
mean and daily GDD; retain accepted source decimal precision. Sum the window
without early daily rounding. Quantize only the final feature to 12 decimal
places using ROUND_HALF_EVEN. Units are °C·days, not temperature alone.

Four models must use one common comparable row set. GDD missingness cannot
remove rows only from M2/M3 while retaining them in M0/M1.

`SEASON_TO_DATE_ENABLED=false`: no authoritative bloom, fruit-set or budbreak
anchor is available. Business-season accumulation would mix calendar progress
and the business-window definition, while M0 already includes season progress
and calendar sin/cos features. First-stage research is therefore rolling only.
Do not introduce extra windows or phenology-anchored features after seeing scores.

## What GDD adds, and what remains unauthorized

GDD is a deterministic transformation of existing temperature observations.
It adds a nonlinear, agronomically motivated **feature representation**, not
new observed data. Its incremental value must eventually be measured against
the same controlled four-model comparison and S0 decision gates.
