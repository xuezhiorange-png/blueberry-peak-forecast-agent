## 1. Version objective

V0.9 establishes a scientifically grounded and auditable blueberry biological forecast foundation using a mechanistic skeleton with parameters that may later be calibrated from authorized data. Its completion means that scientific authority, causal and state contracts, a deterministic theoretical simulator, structural validation, identifiability analysis, future observation requirements, and a prospective protocol exist. It does not mean production accuracy or production readiness has been established.

The verified task sequence is the frozen version plan, S0 global scientific authority review, S1 biological-state and management-event contracts, S2 enterprise data observability audit, S3 theoretical growth simulator, S4 structural validation and identifiability, and this closeout. ## 3. S0 scientific foundation

S0 reviewed 47 scientific sources, including 41 peer-reviewed sources, across 11 countries or production regions. The authority distribution is Tier A 37, A2 4, B 1, C 5, D 0, X 0. The review classified 24 mechanisms as ready for qualitative contract treatment and quarantined six not-ready mechanisms. It found 22 context-dependent mechanisms, four explicit model abstractions, three insufficient-evidence items, one conflicting-evidence item, and zero mechanisms supported for unconditional generalization across all blueberry types, cultivars, climates, and production systems.

The zero universal-mechanism count is not a research failure. It records that the reviewed evidence does not justify removing cultivar, climate, or production-system scope restrictions. Literature values remain literature evidence or priors, not production parameters.

## 6. S3 theoretical architecture

S3 implements 16 modules and 30 equations: three direct literature equations, seven literature-derived structures, two standard agronomic models, and 18 explicit abstractions. The theoretical chain is:

Previous-season crop load → postharvest state → structure and vegetative recovery → flower-bud formation → deciduous dormancy/chilling/forcing or the separate evergreen pathway → bloom progression and bloom cohorts → pruning and flower-thinning effects → pollination → fruit set and crop load → source–sink interaction → fruit cohorts → fruit development → maturity distribution → daily newly mature quantity → Harvest State Engine.

The model supports the three production-system pathways; structural and postharvest state; flower-bud development; three pluggable chilling interpretations; forcing; continuous bloom progression; pollination and fruit set; crop load; source–sink proxies; three fruit-growth curve families; maturity distributions; cohort lineage; and interseason carry-over. Season biological yield is emergent: the sum of daily newly mature mass from modeled fruit cohorts. Area scales plant population; the simulator does not inject an empirical season-total target into a daily curve.

Pruning changes plant structure, fruiting wood, bud potential, source structure, and future shoot potential. Flower thinning changes effective reproductive sink and is not equivalent to pruning. Forcing changes effective microclimate and thermal accumulation; it is not a calendar harvest-date offset. The V0.8 global pooled-yield model remains an empirical reference, not a biological model comparator in this version.

IRRIGATION_STRESS and NUTRITION_INTERVENTION are registered event types but currently have EXPLICIT_NO_EFFECT_TRACE behavior. Their physiological effects are not simulated and are not claimed as implemented capabilities.

## 8. Sensitivity and identifiability

The deterministic Morris elementary-effect screen varied 24 parameters over declared synthetic or literature-scoped ranges. Thirteen were high leverage, three medium, zero low, and eight negligible within the tested ranges; eight parameter/output interactions were flagged as suspected. This is a within-range influence screen, not evidence that a parameter value is correct and not a parameter-selection result.

Structural identifiability was zero; conditional identifiability 19, confounded 24, observation-dependent 37, literature-prior-only 22, and unidentifiable in V1 12. These diagnostic dimensions overlap and must not be summed as mutually exclusive categories. Zero structurally identifiable parameters means that simulator outputs alone cannot uniquely recover all model parameters; targeted physiological observations and parameter provenance are required.

Of 18 explicit abstractions, S4 accepted three for the bounded V1 simulator, restricted 13 to documented domains, reclassified EQA028 (fruit-growth curve-family alternatives) as research-only, and required new authority for EQA029 (dormancy-break treatment efficacy). No critical unresolved abstraction remained. The 13 domain restrictions remain binding; no abstraction is silently promoted to a universal or production law.

## 9. Future calibration requirements

2. Plant density, tree age, productive shoots/canes, and flower-bud census.
3. Business-confirmed production-system assignment.
4. Executed pruning date, type, intensity, and scope.
5. Greenhouse close/open and heating start/stop events with intensity.
6. Calibrated hourly greenhouse air temperature with timestamps and quality/gap flags.
7. Standardized dormancy/budbreak observation anchor.
8. 10%, 50%, and 90% bloom dates with denominator and observation method.
9. Effective-flower and fruit-set sample with denominator.
10. Tagged bloom cohort with repeated fruit stage/count/weight observations through color break and ripe.
11. Authoritative daily harvest quantity and operational exclusions, kept distinct from biological maturity truth.

## 11. Limitations and prospective protocol

No enterprise data were used for fitting; no historical harvest backtest, accuracy scoring, or 2025–2026 model/parameter selection was performed. Production parameter count is zero. Greenhouse microclimate, cultivar-to-area binding, executed management events, direct phenology anchors, flower/fruit-set observations, and cohort tracking remain material data gaps. The simulator's reference scenarios are synthetic and cannot be interpreted as local production forecasts.

The prospective protocol is: authorized calibration observations → parameter calibration → parameter freeze → model artifact seal → future environment and management inputs → forecast issuance → prediction seal → future actuals → locked scoring. Actuals must not exist before prediction sealing. V0.10 prospective validation is reserved but not authorized.
